Is it native or invasive?

Point a webcam at a plant and a Raspberry Pi tells you what it is, whether it belongs in South Florida, and how to double-check. Built on AlexNet, retrained on 25 plants you actually run into in Broward County yards, parks and canal banks.

How it works

AlexNet is the convolutional neural network that won the 2012 ImageNet challenge. It already knows how to see edges, veins, leaf shapes and textures from 1.2 million everyday photos. This project keeps those layers and retrains only the last part of the network to name Broward plants, which is why a few hundred photos per species is enough and why it can run on a Raspberry Pi.

  1. Collect photos

    fetch_photos.py downloads research-grade, Creative Commons photos from iNaturalist, Broward County first, then the rest of Florida. collect.py adds your own webcam shots.

  2. Fine-tune AlexNet

    train.py freezes AlexNet's convolutional layers, retrains its classifier on the 25 species, balances rare and common plants, and keeps the best epoch.

  3. Snap a plant

    identify.py grabs a webcam frame on the Pi, resizes it to 224 × 224 and runs it through the network in about a second.

  4. Native or invasive

    It prints the species, its status and FISC category, ID tips to confirm by hand, and a lookalike warning. Low-confidence guesses say UNSURE instead.

Build one

Parts

  • Raspberry Pi 4 (4 GB or more) or Pi 5, with 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS
  • microSD card, 32 GB or larger
  • USB webcam, ideally autofocus (Logitech C920 or similar) for close-up leaves
  • Official Pi power supply, or a USB-C power bank for field use
  • A laptop or desktop for downloading photos and training (much faster than the Pi)

Tips for good photos

  • Fill the frame with leaves, flowers or fruit, not sky or pavement.
  • Shoot in shade or on overcast days; harsh sun washes out leaf color.
  • Take 20 to 50 of your own webcam photos per species. They help more than extra internet photos.

Commands

On a laptop, download photos and train:

pip install -r requirements.txt
python fetch_photos.py --other 300
python train.py --epochs 15 --site

On the Pi, set up once, copy the model over, then identify:

bash setup_pi.sh && sudo reboot
scp models/alexnet_plants.pt pi@plantid.local:~/broward-plant-id/models/
source venv/bin/activate
python identify.py

Full instructions are in the README.

What it can't do

  • It only knows the 25 plants on its list. Anything else gets forced into the closest match, unless you train the optional "other" class from random Broward plants.
  • Some differences are invisible to a camera. Native Boston fern and invasive tuberous sword fern differ mostly by tubers on the roots. Treat those results as "check the roots".
  • It's a helper, not an authority. Confirm an ID before removing anything, and never remove plants on public or protected land without permission. UF/IFAS Broward County Extension can confirm IDs.
